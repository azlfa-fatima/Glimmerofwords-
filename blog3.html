And the Winners Are… Glimmer of Poets Contest 2025

A Small Honest Review

How the results came to be, and what it took to find the winners

First — thank you. Thank you for sending your poems, for trusting this little contest with your words, and for letting me read the tender, fierce, playful, and solemn corners of your hearts. The response was more than I expected, and reading each entry felt like being invited into a thousand intimate rooms. I wanted to honor every voice that knocked.

Judging was more overwhelming than I thought it would be.

I spent long nights reading, re-reading, and scribbling notes. There were times when the stack of entries felt like a soft, heavy mountain — beautiful but hard to climb. I wrestled with doubt: “Am I fair? Did I miss the heart of this line?” Each poem deserved time and attention, and I wanted to give it that time.

Practically, judging was a careful ritual: I created a scoring sheet, read each poem at least twice, took breaks between batches so my impressions could stay honest, and compared notes against the criteria I set (originality, voice, emotional impact, and craft). Emotionally, it was a lesson in humility — every piece taught me something and reminded me that art resists simple ranking.

That said — decisions had to be made. I read until dawn, I paused when I felt fatigued, and I returned with fresh eyes. I balanced feeling with structure, and in the end I chose three winners whose poems kept returning to me long after the page was closed. Choosing them was painful and beautiful at the same time.

If your poem wasn’t among the top three, please remember: this result does not measure your worth or the power of your voice. It only reflects the small, imperfect space of a single contest. I am humbled and grateful for every submission.

🥇 1st Place — Adeeba Siddiqui

I don’t know how to explain things because I don’t like betraying them

To explain a poem is to betray it For it is to be felt. So do my soul justice and let me dwell. In the eyes of mind, and in the distance between you and I, I have found strange solace. One may think I am talking about a lover, but no lover has ever been loved by me, whatsoever. Call me a bad person if you will, but avoiding things last so long, Before they crack and disintegrate into oblivion. like in many other things, I have found myself keeping a distance. To not hurt, or rather, to not get hurt. I have never known. So I write to feel, but I can't share what I feel in my writing. Because to explain something, is to betray what it stands for. No good thing, having meaning whatsoever, deserves loosing it to someone like some question with an answer. Not everything deserves to have an answer.

🥈 2nd Place — Jawad Hijazi

Though Murmurs Whisper

A shadow lingers etched in time's dark scroll, A past that clings, a story yet untold. The heart, a clipped wing dreamer, bound my memory's evermore chains. A wounded knight with burdens fierce to restrain. Though murmurs whisper forth the future, bright and bold, A hand outstretched, a story yet to unfold. The past's embrace, a heavy, chilling shroud, Yet hope's flame still flickers proudly, bravely vowed. The echoes linger of tears, laughter, and strife, A pastry of moments woven through life. But present's proclaim, a beckoning, gentle plea, To break free from the shackles wild and free, The future beckons with a promise softly sung, A wounded warrior, onward, bravely flung. Though scars remain, a testament to pain, A strength within, to rise and conquer again, The hand reached out, to grasp the sunlit shore, To leave the shadows, and forevermore, Embrace the dawn, and let the darkness cease, A new beginning for finding inner peace. How does it feel to be haunted by life? I want to move on like the night sun, but the moon keeps calling me to rise again. I want to hide in the night yet experience the light, I want to travel far away, but the traces of my memories won't let me. I want to erase my love for you, but how can I erase the heart that beats for you?

🥉 3rd Place — Dr. Sarvani

The Shape of Our Thoughts

Aren’t we shaped by what we’ve lived through? Woven from moments we never chose— and some we chose to survive. We carry beliefs like hand-me-down coats, some warm, some heavy, some never really ours. We feel anxious— because once, worry kept us safe. We feel joy— because it reminded us we were still alive. We speak in anger— because it once gave voice to pain we didn’t know how to name. Our emotions… they’re echoes— soft ripples from stones thrown long ago. Yesterday whispers in today’s choices, and today sketches the shape of our tomorrow. But what if— beneath all the noise— it’s not the feeling, but the thought, that moves the hand? What if one thought, held gently, could loosen an old knot? Shift the weight? Open a window? So— breathe. Let this moment be new. Let one kinder thought find space in you. Because you are not just what happened. You are also what’s becoming.
